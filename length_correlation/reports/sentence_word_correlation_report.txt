Articles from Yes and No pairs have a similar individual structural size.
Yes articles contain an average of 142.6378 words, while No articles contain an average of 139.1911 words.
The difference between the two classes is 3.4467 words per article.

The difference between the articles inside each pair is more informative than the individual article length.
The average word-count difference is 38.0306 words for Yes pairs and 45.646 words for No pairs.
Therefore, No pairs differ by approximately 7.6154 more words than Yes pairs.

The average number of sentences per article is almost identical between the two classes.
Yes articles contain an average of 5.8103 sentences, while No articles contain an average of 5.8607 sentences.

Yes pairs have a slightly smaller sentence-count difference.
The average sentence-count difference is 2.6693 for Yes pairs and 3.0264 for No pairs.
No pairs differ by approximately 0.3571 more sentences than Yes pairs.

Pairs with exactly the same number of sentences represent 20.82% of Yes pairs and 17.62% of No pairs.
The percentage is 3.2 percentage points higher for Yes pairs.

Most pairs differ by only a small number of sentences.
The distributions of Yes and No pairs overlap considerably, which means that sentence count alone cannot reliably determine whether two articles describe the same event.

Overall, Yes pairs tend to have more similar word counts and sentence counts than No pairs.
However, these structural differences are moderate and should be used as auxiliary features together with lexical, semantic and temporal similarity.
